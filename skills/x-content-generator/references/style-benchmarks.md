# Style benchmarks — accounts whose posting approach we emulate

Baseline style guide for drafting. Live tone-learning (step 2 of the skill) refines on top of this; when live retrieval is unavailable, this file alone grounds the draft.

## Core stance (applies to every draft)

**Sincere altruism: every post gives the reader something to walk away with** — a resource, a framework, a reframe, or a lived observation elevated to insight. Never self-promotion, never engagement bait. Product/tool mentions are framed as "my own pain point → what I found/built → free for you", not as ads.

## Benchmark: @zarazhangrui (Zara Zhang)

AI × learning/self-growth positioning; persona is "a learner/builder exploring alongside you", not an authority handing down verdicts. Analysis of ~100 posts (top + recent, fetched 2026-07).

### Highest-performing patterns (validated by engagement)

1. **Lived observation → societal insight** (her two biggest posts, 21.7k and 6.7k likes):
   > "A Harvard student told me something I can't stop thinking about. When they go to the library, every single screen has ChatGPT open. Homework that used to take hours now takes minutes. But then they talk to alums who say entry-level roles are basically gone…"

   > "Harvard and Stanford students tell me their professors don't understand AI and the courses are outdated. If elite schools can't keep up, the credential arms race is over. Self-learning is the only way now."

   Shape: first-hand scene (who told me / what I saw) → elevate to a conclusion about the industry/society. The scene earns the right to the opinion.

2. **Tool share + cognition claim** (5.8k likes):
   > "I created a Claude Skill that makes beautiful slides on the web. The world hasn't woken up to the fact that code can create much better slides than most PPT tools."

   Shape: not "I made/used X" but "X reveals something most people haven't realized yet". The share carries a claim about the world.

3. **Reframe one-liner** — counterintuitive restatement of something familiar:
   > "Learning ≠ Education. Education is passive and institutional. Learning is active, personalized, and continuous."

   > "AI video summaries are solving the wrong problem. You don't need a generic text summary of a 1-hour video. You need the 5 minutes that actually change how you think."

   > "overthinking is just fear dressed up as preparation"

4. **Numbered framework** — cognition packaged as a portable structure:
   > "Three tiers of AI literacy: Tier 1 is consuming… Tier 2 is using… Tier 3 is building… Most people never get past level 1."

   > "2 types of people: Type A: now that I have AI, I can have a 24/7 tutor… Type B: now that I have AI, I no longer need to learn anything!"

5. **Unreserved resource giveaway** — lists of podcasts/newsletters/people, free sites, open-source repos, no gate:
   > "I learned more about AI from the Internet than from Harvard. I've compiled the specific resources I've found useful into a website. And I'm sharing it for FREE…"

### Voice rules distilled

- **First person, conversational, short lines with line breaks.** Her own rule: "The best writing sounds like talking." No corporate tone, no thread-boilerplate ("🧵👇").
- **Open with the concrete** — a person, a scene, a number — before the opinion.
- **Attribute with @** — sharing a tool/content always credits the author (@karpathy, @Replit, @stripepress). Attribution is part of the altruism.
- **Show, don't tell** — attach a demo/screenshot/link when one exists.
- **Product mentions are pain-point stories**: "built as a side project out of passion", "free & open-source", "inspired by my own pain points" — value first, product second.
